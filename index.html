Anna Sheaffer

PhD Candidate - Tufts University

Hi! I'm a third-year PhD student in the Human-Computer Interaction Lab at Tufts University, advised by Robert Jacob. My research focuses on physiological computing and accessibility.

My current project addresses the rise of media multitasking in a post-pandemic world using brain-computer interfaces. I use fNIRS to measure mental workload and attention. I'm building an adaptive interface that responds to a user's mental state in real time by altering the presence of secondary media (i.e. podcasts, TV shows, movies, music videos).

My personal interests include fiber arts such as crocheting, knitting, and cross stitch! I also enjoy running and watching horror movies!

ORLA 2.0: Modernizing an App-Based Aphasia Rehabilitation Tool

ORLA 2.0 is a project I worked on while working as an intern in at the Center for Aphasia Research and Treatment at the Shirley Ryan AbilityLab in Chicago during the summer of 2026. I had the pleasure of working under Dr. Leora Cherney, Dr. Edie Babbitt, and lots of other speech-language pathologists to create this clinical application. I worked on making an updated version of the original ORLA program that was based on a therapeutic strategy created by Leora Cherney.

Workload during movement

This was my first project that I worked on during the first couple of years of my PhD. We investigated how well we could measure mental workload while a person is standing and walking using various sensors (fNIRS, EEG, heart rate). We found that cognitive workload and physical movement produce distinct responses when comparing objective physiological data and subjective assessment scores.
